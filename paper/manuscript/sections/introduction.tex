\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

% 在科学写作评价中，判断往往依赖领域知识和多个评价维度，

LLM-as-a-judge has become a widely used paradigm for evaluating open-ended generations;
to support criterion-grounded judgments and make final scores more interpretable and auditable,
many methods elicit rationales---explicit natural-language justifications
grounded in the evaluation criteria---before assigning a score,
a design we refer to as \emph{rationale-first}
~\citep{liu-etal-2023-g,kim-etal-2024-prometheus-iclr}.
In scientific-writing evaluation, where judgments hinge on
domain knowledge and multiple quality dimensions,
this design is particularly relevant
~\citep{sadallah-etal-2025-good,sahinuc-etal-2026-reward,zhang-etal-2026-surveyreview}.

Existing evaluation methods have introduced rationales through prompting,
supervised distillation and reinforcement learning,
and have generally reported higher human correlation, judge agreement or generalization ability
~\citep{liu-etal-2023-g,kim-etal-2024-prometheus-iclr,sahinuc-etal-2026-reward}.
These results suggest that explicit rationales can help models organize textual evidence
and scoring decisions under complex evaluation criteria.
However, existing experiments often change rationale generation together with prompt format,
training objective, optimization strategy and inference protocol,
making it difficult to determine whether the gains come from rationales themselves
or from the full evaluation configuration.
For example, the gains of G-Eval depend jointly on evaluation-step prompting and its scoring mechanism;
the improvements of SciRM also combine domain-preference optimization,
reasoning/self-verification training and rationale-first outputs,
leaving the independent effects of rationale supervision and rationale-first inference unclear
~\citep{liu-etal-2023-g,sahinuc-etal-2026-reward}.

% 现有归因研究考察了这两种设置，

% ~\citep{wadhwa-etal-2024-investigating,shi-etal-2025-investigating}。
% 而在科学写作评价中，模型需要整合领域知识、权衡开放式文本证据、
% 并将多维 rubric 标准映射到序数量表上。

The concern is compounded by evidence from other task families:
rationale augmentation can degrade task performance
~\citep{zhu-etal-2025-rationales},
while CoT gains are concentrated mainly in mathematical or symbolic reasoning tasks
and remain inconsistent elsewhere
~\citep{sprague-etal-2025-cot}.
To determine whether performance changes arise from learning rationales during training
or from generating rationales before scoring at inference time
---the two factors we term \emph{rationale supervision} and \emph{rationale-first inference}---
existing attribution studies have examined both settings,
but almost exclusively on tasks outside scientific-writing evaluation,
such as general reasoning and NLU
~\citep{wadhwa-etal-2024-investigating,shi-etal-2025-investigating}.
In scientific-writing evaluation, the model must instead integrate
domain knowledge, weigh open-ended textual evidence, and map
multidimensional rubric criteria onto an ordinal scale.

\paragraph{Our work and contributions.}
We investigate how rationale supervision and generation affect
scientific-writing evaluation, and use these insights to examine
training configurations supporting both direct scoring and rationale-first inference.
Our contributions are threefold.

\textbf{(C1) Controlled experimental design.}
We cross score-only (SO) and rationale-and-score (RS) supervision
with direct-score (DS) and rationale-first (RF) inference
in a $2\times2$ factorial design across seven tasks.
Experiments cover Qwen3-4B and Qwen3-0.6B,
with 28 conditions per backbone and three random seeds per condition,
allowing us to examine supervision effects, inference effects,
and their interaction.

\textbf{(C2) Diagnosing rationale quality and scoring performance.}
On Qwen3-4B, we combine teacher-forced rationale NLL,
rationale quality and consistency assessment,
paired prediction transitions, and sentence-level error annotation.
These analyses show that RS improves rationale fitting and quality
without improving scoring.
Among 158 selected cases where DS is correct but RF makes a severe error,
correcting the rationales and rescoring with fixed prefixes recovers the
gold score in 142 cases under the original protocol; excluding two post hoc
rubric-consistency flags yields 140 recoveries among 156 cases.

\textbf{(C3) Training for both inference interfaces.}
Building on prior work on separately normalized
losses~\citep{shi-etal-2025-investigating} and rationale
regeneration~\citep{chiang-etal-2025-tract}, we instantiate PaIR and ReaSon and
integrate them into RePaIR. We evaluate these configurations under
both DS and RF inference. RePaIR improves mean QWK over SO across the four
ordinal tasks at both model sizes under both interfaces.
