\paragraph{RQ1: How Do SO and RS Differ in Scoring Performance and Rationale Modeling?}

% Figure~2 展示了四个序数任务上的 QWK。采用 score-only training
% 时，direct-score 和 rationale-first inference 的平均 QWK 分别为

Figure~\ref{fig:training-targets-by-task-type} (left) reports QWK on the 
four ordinal tasks. With score-only training, 
direct-score and rationale-first inference achieve mean QWK scores of 0.747 and 0.712, 
respectively. With rationale-and-score training, the corresponding scores are 0.696 and 0.679. 
This pattern is consistent across tasks: score-only training outperforms rationale-and-score 
training, and direct-score inference outperforms rationale-first inference.

% Figure~3 展示了二分类任务上的 Macro-F1。采用 score-only training
% 时，direct-score 和 rationale-first inference 的平均结果接近，
% 分别为 0.928 和 0.923。采用 rationale-and-score training 时，
% rationale-first inference 的平均 Macro-F1 为 0.922，明显高于
% direct-score inference 的 0.843。因此，二分类任务中的训练效果
% 取决于推理接口：score-only training 仅在 direct-score inference
% 下表现出明显优势。

Figure~\ref{fig:training-targets-by-task-type} (right) reports Macro-F1 on the binary 
tasks. With score-only training, direct-score and 
rationale-first inference perform similarly, scoring 0.928 and 0.923, respectively. 
With rationale-and-score training, rationale-first inference reaches 0.922, compared 
with 0.843 under direct-score inference. The effect of the training objective therefore 
depends on the inference protocol, with a clear score-only advantage appearing only under 
direct-score inference.

\textbf{Rationale supervision improves teacher-rationale fitting, but this
advantage does not translate into better scoring.}
We measure teacher-rationale modeling using teacher-forced rationale NLL.
Table~\ref{tab:rationale-nll} reports the task-level NLL results for Base,
score-only training, and rationale-and-score training, where lower values indicate
closer fitting to the teacher-rationale distribution.
As shown in Table~\ref{tab:rationale-nll}, the original Qwen3-4B without
task-specific fine-tuning (Base) obtains a mean NLL of 3.4480,
which decreases to 1.6964 with score-only training and 0.7793
with rationale-and-score training.
Rationale-and-score training achieves the lowest NLL on every task,
showing that direct rationale supervision improves teacher-token prediction
but does not guarantee better scoring performance.

Table~\ref{tab:rationale-quality-consistency} reports the overall preference results,
scores across four rationale-quality dimensions, and rationale--score consistency rates.
As shown in Table~\ref{tab:rationale-quality-consistency}, rationales from
rationale-and-score training were preferred in 248 cases (70.9\%).

evidence grounding, key-information coverage, and unsupported-claim control,
with the largest difference in key-information coverage. Its mean score across the four
dimensions was 2.832, compared with 2.400 for score-only training, and its rationale--score
consistency was also higher at 96.6\% versus 74.3\%.

To characterize how the inference protocol affects ordinal scoring, we
paired direct-score and rationale-first predictions for the same samples
under both supervision objectives, yielding 11,364 prediction transitions.
Predictions were categorized as correct, adjacent errors, severe errors,
or format-invalid. Table~\ref{tab:ordinal-interface-changes} summarizes
the average changes after switching from direct-score to rationale-first
inference.

With score-only training, QWK decreased by 0.035, accuracy by 3.52
percentage points, and the severe-error rate increased by 3.98 points.
With rationale-and-score training, accuracy increased by 3.49 points,
but adjacent errors decreased by 7.51 points while severe errors increased
by 4.26 points, resulting in a QWK decrease of 0.017. Because QWK
penalizes distant errors more heavily, the shift toward severe errors
offset the gain in exact predictions. Invalid-output rates changed by
less than 0.25 percentage points under both training objectives.

\textbf{RF rationales closely align with prediction transitions, and
their errors often emerge early.}
Across 11,364 DS-to-RF prediction pairs per objective, score-only
training produced 377 harmful and 151 beneficial transitions, whereas
rationale-and-score training produced 338 and 269. Judging was
completed for 1,119 of 1,135 selected cases, with detailed counts
reported in Appendix A.

Among 701 harmful transitions, 700 rationales supported an incorrect
score. In contrast, all 418 beneficial-transition rationales supported
the gold score. This result is consistent with the rationale--score
alignment observed in RQ1.

Figure~\ref{fig:rationale-error-analysis} summarizes the types and
positions of sentence-level errors. Across the two training conditions,
rubric misapplication (37.8\%) and score-mapping errors (18.3\%) were
the two most common types among 1,115 definitively labeled error
sentences, together accounting for 56.1\%, which indicates that errors
primarily arise from applying the rubric and mapping the resulting
judgment to a score. When the two training conditions are combined,
74.9\% of first errors occur in the first half of the rationale,
including 38.8\% in the first quarter, suggesting that errors typically
emerge early and may continue to shape the subsequent reasoning and
final score.

\textbf{Rationale correction substantially improved scoring in the
selected harmful cases.}
FixOrig reproduced the NatRF prediction in 157 of 158 cases, yielding
a 99.37\% reproduction rate. Both settings produced valid scores, and
their MAE values were nearly identical (2.038 versus 2.044), indicating
that fixed-prefix rescoring rarely changed the original prediction.
Full task-level reproduction results are provided in Appendix A.

Relative to FixOrig, FixCorr changed all 158 predictions. As shown in
Table~\ref{tab:rationale-correction}, 142 cases (89.87\%) returned to
the gold score, while the remaining 16 improved from severe to adjacent
errors. MAE decreased from 2.044 to 0.101. Among the 157 cases in which
FixOrig reproduced NatRF, 141 (89.81\%) recovered, showing that the
aggregate improvement was not driven by the single reproduction
failure. Recovery was observed across all evaluated task--training groups,
showing that rationale correction consistently guided the model toward
more accurate scores. The lower recovery rate for Verifiability
indicates that the magnitude of improvement varies across tasks.
Overall, erroneous rationale content was an important source of harmful
predictions, and correcting it substantially improved subsequent scoring.

% \subsection{RQ3：如何同时保持 rationale 质量与评分性能？}
\paragraph{RQ3: How Can RS Preserve Rationale Quality and Scoring Performance?}

% 针对前文发现的 rationale 来源问题和训练--推理接口失配问题，
% 我们分别提出 ReaSon 和 PaIR：前者调整 rationale 监督的来源，
% 后者通过配对接口监督改善 DS 与 RF 的适配。由于两种方法解决的
% 问题具有互补性，我们进一步将其结合为 RePaIR。图
% \ref{fig:rq3-method-results} 展示了三种方法在相同推理接口下
% 相对于标准 RS 的序数 QWK 和二分类 Macro-F1 变化。总体而言，
% RePaIR 在保留 DS 性能的同时取得了最大的 RF 序数评分提升，
% 在两个推理接口间表现出最均衡的整体性能。

To address the rationale-source problem and the mismatch between training
and inference interfaces identified above, we introduced ReaSon and PaIR.
ReaSon modifies the source of rationale supervision, whereas PaIR improves
adaptation to both DS and RF through paired-interface supervision. Because
the two methods target complementary aspects of RS, we further combined
them into RePaIR. Figure~\ref{fig:rq3-method-results} shows the changes in
ordinal QWK and binary Macro-F1 for all three methods relative to standard
RS under the same inference protocol. Overall, RePaIR retained DS
performance while achieving the largest improvement in RF ordinal scoring,
providing the most balanced performance across the two inference protocols.

% \textbf{PaIR 通过配对接口监督同时改善了接口覆盖和分支学习。}
% PaIR 在 DS 下取得 0.757 的序数 QWK、0.476 的 MAE 和
% 0.934 的分类 Macro-F1，在 RF 下分别达到 0.698、0.555
% 和 0.929，且两个接口的严格格式有效率均为 100\%。相较于
% 使用相同配对数据的 Mix，PaIR 在 DS 和 RF 下分别将 QWK
% 提高 0.015 和 0.008，将 MAE 降低 0.022 和 0.016，并将
% Macro-F1 提高 0.010 和 0.004。这一对照表明，接口配对解决了
% DS 覆盖问题，分支级归一化则进一步提升了评分效果。

\textbf{PaIR improves both interface coverage and branch learning
through paired supervision.}
PaIR achieved ordinal QWK, MAE, and classification Macro-F1 scores of
0.757, 0.476, and 0.934 under DS, and 0.698, 0.555, and 0.929 under
RF, with 100\% strict-format validity at both interfaces. Relative to
Mix using the same paired data, PaIR increased QWK by 0.015 and 0.008,
reduced MAE by 0.022 and 0.016, and increased Macro-F1 by 0.010 and
0.004 under DS and RF, respectively. These results show that paired
supervision provides DS coverage, while branch-wise normalization
offers an additional scoring benefit.

% \textbf{RePaIR 联合了 ReaSon 和 PaIR 的互补收益。}
% RePaIR 在 DS 下取得 0.758 的 QWK、0.476 的 MAE 和
% 0.931 的 Macro-F1，与 PaIR 的 0.757、0.476 和 0.934
% 基本一致。在 RF 下，RePaIR 将 PaIR 的 QWK 从 0.698
% 提高到 0.735，并将 MAE 从 0.555 降至 0.502，而
% Macro-F1 仅由 0.929 变为 0.926。两个接口均保持 100\%
% 的严格格式有效率。因此，RePaIR 保留了 PaIR 的 DS 能力，
% 同时将 rationale 重生成带来的收益集中体现于 RF 序数评分，
% 从而在两个接口间取得更均衡的表现。

\textbf{RePaIR combines the complementary gains of ReaSon and PaIR.}
Under DS, RePaIR achieved a QWK of 0.758, an MAE of 0.476, and a
Macro-F1 of 0.931, closely matching PaIR at 0.757, 0.476, and 0.934.
Under RF, RePaIR increased PaIR's QWK from 0.698 to 0.735 and reduced
MAE from 0.555 to 0.502, while Macro-F1 changed only from 0.929 to
0.926. Both interfaces retained 100\% strict-format validity. RePaIR
therefore preserves PaIR's DS capability while concentrating the
benefit of rationale regeneration on RF ordinal scoring, producing
more balanced performance across the two interfaces.
