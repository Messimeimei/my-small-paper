\begin{abstract}
Eliciting rationales before scoring has become a common practice
in LLM-based scientific-writing evaluation,
supporting interpretable, rubric-grounded judgments;
yet prior work often changes prompt formats, training objectives, and inference protocols together,
leaving the gains hard to attribute to rationales themselves.
We therefore build a 2$\times$2 factorial framework
that crosses score-only supervision (SO) and rationale-and-score supervision (RS)
with direct-score (DS) and rationale-first (RF) inference,
yielding 28 conditions over seven scientific-writing tasks
analyzed via rationale NLL, rationale-quality assessment, DS--RF prediction transitions,
and sentence-level error annotation.
Experiments yield three findings:
(1) RS improves rationale fitting and quality but not scoring,
while RF increases severe errors and lowers QWK on ordinal tasks.
(2) Rationales in harmful transitions mostly support incorrect scores,
mainly through rubric misapplication and score mapping;
correcting them restores the gold score in 142 of 158 cases.
(3) We further propose RePaIR, which attains the highest ordinal QWK
under both interfaces (DS: 0.696$\rightarrow$0.758; RF: 0.679$\rightarrow$0.735)
with 100\% strict-format validity.
These results indicate that rationale quality is not a reliable proxy for scoring ability,
and supervision objectives should be co-designed with the inference protocol.
\end{abstract}
