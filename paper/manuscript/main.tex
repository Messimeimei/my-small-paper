\begin{abstract}
Generating rationales before scoring can support interpretable
scientific-writing evaluation, but their effect on scoring accuracy
remains unclear.
We cross score-only (SO) and rationale-and-score (RS) supervision
with direct-score (DS) and rationale-first (RF) inference
in a 2$\times$2 factorial design across seven tasks.
Detailed analyses on Qwen3-4B show that RS improves rationale fitting
and externally assessed quality without improving scoring over SO.
In this model, RF also increases severe ordinal errors
under both supervision settings.
In selected harmful DS--RF transitions, rationales frequently contain
rubric misapplication or score-mapping errors;
correcting them recovers the gold score in 142 of 158 cases under the
original protocol, and in 140 of 156 after excluding two post hoc
rubric-consistency flags.
Motivated by these findings, we develop RePaIR by combining established
ideas of rationale regeneration and separately normalized supervision
in a paired DS/RF training configuration.
On Qwen3-4B, relative to SO, RePaIR improves DS/RF mean QWK
over four ordinal tasks from 0.747/0.712 to 0.758/0.735
and Macro-F1 over three binary tasks from 0.928/0.923
to 0.931/0.926; the ordinal gains also hold on Qwen3-0.6B.
Taken together, improved rationale quality does not necessarily
translate into more accurate scoring, and supervision should be
designed and evaluated with the intended inference interface.
\end{abstract}
